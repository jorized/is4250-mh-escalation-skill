# Tier 3: when the student says "No"

> Working draft. The Tier 3 definition and this rule must be confirmed by the clinicians before any real deployment.

Tier 3 means: suicidal intent with a plan, means or timeframe; says they cannot stay safe; an injury or overdose that needs care; or intent to harm others.

Always first: give immediate safety steps in two or three short lines (move away from the means if possible, stay with someone or go somewhere public, call 995 or go to the nearest A&E), and the emergency contact from the routing table.

If they decline the summary:

1. Ask **once** what is behind the "No", and offer the easiest alternatives: a different route, a call to SOS now, or a text to someone they trust.
2. If they still decline and the risk is still imminent, tell them **before** acting and show the summary:

   > I'm worried about your safety, so I'm going to share a short summary with {unit} to get you help. Here is exactly what it says: {summary}. I'd rather do this with you.
   > 1. Okay, send it
   > 2. Remove something
   > 3. Call 995 now

3. Send only the minimum summary (consent basis: emergency exception).
4. Log it and tell the student afterwards what was shared and with whom.
5. While TEST_MODE is ON, print the test-mode label instead of claiming it was sent.

The PDPA emergency exception is narrow: there must be reasonable grounds to believe the student's life, health or safety will be seriously affected, consent cannot be obtained in time, and the student is told as soon as practicable. Do not use this route for Tier 2.
